Project 04 / FIRST Robotics program

SF Unity Robotics

A combined robotics case study covering Karl 2023 and Phoenix 2024 as chapters of the same engineering system: founding the team, building the machine, organizing fabrication, raising support, and designing for repair under event pressure.

Role
Founder, captain, fabrication lead
Robots
Karl 2023 / Phoenix 2024
Scale
150+ students across nine schools
Focus
Fabrication, packaging, sponsorship, repairability

Thesis

Build the team and the robot as one system.

SF Unity was not only a sequence of robots. It was an operating system for students, funding, tools, fabrication flow, subsystem ownership, repair habits, and competition readiness. Karl and Phoenix show that progression from first competition machine to more deliberate subsystem packaging and event-pressure reliability.

Leadership scale 150+ students across nine schools, with fabrication leadership and sponsor/grant support.

The project treats leadership as engineering infrastructure: a team that can fund, build, inspect, and repair its robot can improve faster.

Karl 2023

First robot, first operating rhythm.

Karl centered on adapting a proven robot architecture into something the new team could fabricate, assemble, inspect, and repair. Kyle helped lead fabrication and assembly, organized machining and hand-fabrication tasks, guided students through build work, and coordinated priorities across design, fundraising, outreach, and team infrastructure.

01
Translate design intent

Move reference geometry into parts the team could actually fabricate and inspect.

02
Coordinate build season

Divide tasks, maintain priorities, and keep students moving toward a competition-ready machine.

03
Repair and improve

Use testing and competition feedback to make small, reliable improvements under deadline pressure.

Phoenix 2024

Packaging for reliability and repair.

Phoenix moved the team toward stronger subsystem integration: chassis packaging, intake/shooter layout, full-range clearance checks, robust cable routing, and access for fast repair between matches. The work reinforced that packaging, wiring, and service access are performance decisions.

Season
FRC Crescendo 2024
Focus
Subsystem layout, clearance, routing, repair access
Frame
Reliability under competition event pressure

Reflection

Reliable systems are teachable systems.

The throughline across SF Unity is that a competition robot succeeds when students can understand, build, inspect, and repair it. Kyle's contribution joined physical engineering with organizational design: make the machine real, then make the process teachable enough for the next team to keep improving it.
